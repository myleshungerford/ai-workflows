# Fact Check Loop

Identify factual and numerical claims in the deliverable. Prioritize claims that affect its conclusion, then check the remaining factual assertions. Do not describe a sample check as an exhaustive audit.

For each claim:

1. Locate the exact source passage, table, row, cell, query, or command output. A citation's presence does not prove it supports the statement.
2. Check definitions, population, time period, units, and scope. A source may support a narrower claim than the draft makes.
3. Independently recompute consequential numbers when possible. Check denominators, rounding, and percentage change versus percentage-point change.
4. Classify the claim as verified against the available source, needs caveat, unsupported, contradicted, or not checkable with current access.
5. If editing is authorized, correct or qualify it and recheck the revised wording. Otherwise provide the exact proposed correction. Do not silently replace missing facts with plausible estimates.

Verification against a source establishes correspondence, not that the source is infallible. Flag material source limitations. Do not use your own generated analysis summary as independent evidence for itself.

Keep a compact claim-to-source record while checking. Example using fictional practice data:

| Claim | Source | Result | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rate rose by 5% | practice.csv, rows A and B: 20% and 25% | Needs correction if describing the absolute difference | Say 5 percentage points; relative increase is 25% |

Report corrections, remaining caveats, and source locations. State the coverage of the check and any inaccessible evidence. Never call a claim verified merely because another agent agreed.
